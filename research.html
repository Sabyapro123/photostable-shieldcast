Research

What patients, pharmacists and dermatologists would tell us

We read the published Indian studies, turned them into a modelled survey panel and opened a real patient survey. This page keeps the three apart, so you always know which numbers are measured and which are modelled.

Read this first. The charts in the next section come from a modelled panel, not fieldwork. Each answer share is set from a published study (tagged Anchored) or from a stated team assumption (tagged Assumed), and then sampled so the panel behaves like a real sample of that size. The code, the seed and every simulated answer are in the survey folder of the repository.
How the panel was built

A published study sets each answer share where one exists, and a stated team assumption fills the rest. The respondents are simulated with a fixed seed, so anyone can rerun the model. Real interviews in the pilot replace every modelled number.

Where differentiation is weak

Three of the four tubes say the same thing on the box

Gold, Acne and Emulgel all carry SPF 55 on the label. The label gives a doctor, a pharmacist or a patient no reason to choose one over another. The reason to choose has to come from somewhere else, which is the job of the Shield Map, Course Rx and Season Switch.

A hidden strength. The ingredient list of Pro+ includes bemotrizinol, the filter the US FDA cleared in June 2026 as its first new sunscreen active in over twenty years. Photostable already has it, yet nothing on the Rx says so.

Global trends and the missing links

What the range needs next

  • Visible-light tints. Iron-oxide tints are advised for melasma (PRISM-ISF 2025). Photostable has no tint under its own name.
  • Mineral after procedures. La Shield Fisico holds this space. ShieldCast logs every such patient to size a Photostable mineral.
  • Reapplication formats. Sticks, mists and powders are growing because they go over sweat and make-up.
  • Verified SPF. BIS made in vivo SPF proof compulsory in 2026, so proof is now a selling point.
  • Connected use. Euromonitor expects QR checks and app reminders to become standard in sun care.
Published evidence we anchored to
  1. Agarwal S.B. et al., Indian Journal of Dermatology, 2018: 2,037 adults at a Mumbai hospital; 61% did not know about sunscreens; users wanted less sticky (73%) products; stickiness (25%) and price (18%) were the named reasons for not using.
  2. Mintel, 2019: 65% of 3,000 Indian adults had not used sunscreen in a year, and 25% did not understand SPF or PA.
  3. Statista and ProdegeMR, 2019: 33% of Indians surveyed used sun care daily.
  4. Upadhyay H. et al., Journal of Clinical and Diagnostic Research, 2021: 70% of 300 medical students did not know the ideal SPF.
  5. KrupaShankar D.S.R. et al., Dermatology and Therapy, 2014: 35% of 331 melasma patients used any sunscreen.
  6. Farberg A.S. et al., JAMA Dermatology, 2017: 99.4% of dermatologists said patients under-apply, and 71% weigh cosmetic elegance.
  7. Vector Consulting Group, 2025: a prescribed brand was unavailable in 10 to 60% of cases across 900+ chemists.
  8. Iyer H.K. and Berad N., Mumbai pilot of 120 prescriptions: 34.8% of dermatology brand requests were substituted.
  9. Hacker E. et al., Preventive Medicine, 2019: UV stickers lifted sunscreen reapplication from 68% to 80%.
  10. Kohli M. et al., PRISM-ISF Delphi consensus, Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology, 2025.
